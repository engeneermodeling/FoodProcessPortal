# Laboratory Work №23. Studying the Process of Change of Drying Agent Parameters in an Air Dryer Using the I–x Diagram

## Theoretical Background

Using only the values of the dry-bulb and wet-bulb temperatures, the process occurring in the dryer, with single use of the drying agent (air), is represented on the I–x diagram as shown in fig. 23.1. First, based on the psychrometer's readings, zero point 0 is constructed, which characterizes the parameters of the outside air at the entrance to the dryer. The psychrometer consists of a dry and a wetted thermometer and serves to determine the air's parameters from the thermometer readings, using special tables or the I–x diagram. Point 0 is found at the intersection of the line of constant dry-bulb temperature t0dry = const with the line I0 = const, drawn from the point of intersection of the wet-bulb temperature line I0 = const with the line of saturated air φ=100%.

The parameters of the air heated to temperature t1 (before entering the dryer) are characterized by point 1. Since the heating of the air occurs at constant moisture content x0 = const, we construct point 1 by drawing from point 0 a vertical line x0 = const to its intersection with the line t1 = const. Point 2, which characterizes the parameters of the air at the exit from the dryer, is constructed similarly to the construction of point 0, based on the dry-bulb t2dry and wet-bulb t2wet temperature readings of the psychrometer that records the air temperature at the exit from the dryer.

Connecting points 0–1 and 1–2, we obtain a graph of the change in air parameters, on which line 1–2 represents the actual drying process. Since during drying, part of the heat supplied to the dryer is lost on heating the material being dried and to the surrounding environment, the angle between line 1–2 and the horizontal exceeds the angle of 45°, at which the lines I = const run.

## Laboratory Setup

Fig. 23.3.

Diagram of the air dryer

The setup, shown in fig. 23.3, is a tube 3, whose internal surface is covered with fabric 4 that is easily moistened. The upper end of the fabric passes from the internal surface of the tube to the external one and is immersed in ring-shaped reservoir 5 with water. Due to capillary wetting, the fabric in the tube becomes moist and remains moist as long as there is water in reservoir 5. The amount of water entering tube 3 depends on its level in reservoir 5. This level is determined by the position of the lower end of tube 7, which supplies water from closed vessel 6 with a measuring scale.

From below, air enters the tube through funnel 2, which is first heated in preheater 1. By changing the position of funnel 2, one can change the ratio of the amount of preheated air and air drawn in through gap 11 between the lower end of the tube and the throat of the funnel.

The degree of air preheating is regulated by changing the load on preheater 1 or the size of gap 11.

The parameters of the air from the tube, of the surrounding air, as well as of the heated and mixed air, are determined using psychrometers 8 and 9 and thermometer 10.

## Procedure

Experiment using only heated air

1. By filling reservoir 5 with water, we moisten the fabric on the internal surface of the tube.

2. We set the funnel and tube without a gap.

3. After the process parameters have stabilized, as evidenced by the unchanging temperatures of the dry and wet thermometers of psychrometer 8, located above the upper end of the tube, we regulate the water level in reservoir 5. It should be such that water does not drip from the lower end of the tube.

4. 10–15 minutes after the water level has stabilized, we record the values of the physical quantities:

duration of the experiment τe, s;

dry t0dry and wet t0wet thermometer temperature for the outside air (using psychrometer 9), °C;

temperature of the preheated air (t1 or t1′ according to the readings of thermometer 10), °C;

dry t2dry and wet t2wet thermometer temperature for the air at the exit from the dryer (according to the readings of psychrometer 8), °C;

mass of moisture removed during the experiment (from the initial and final amounts of water in vessel 6), kg.

Experiment with mixing cold and preheated air

We conduct the experiment using the same procedure as the previous one, but with a certain gap set between the funnel and the tube.

## Processing the Results

1. From the data of the experiment using only heated air, we construct the graph of change of air parameters in the I–x diagram, similar to that shown in fig. 23.1.

2. Using the values of moisture content and enthalpy determined from the graph, we calculate the process characteristics using formulas (23.1)–(23.3).

3. We calculate the specific heat loss qloss, %, to the surrounding environment, as the ratio of heat loss to its total consumption.

4. We determine the amount of water evaporating per unit time from 1 m² of the internal surface of the tube (write the formula independently).

5. We perform analogous construction and calculations using the data of the experiment with mixing cold and preheated air.

6. We analyze the obtained results and record conclusions.

When analyzing the results, one should take into account that the specific air consumption in dryers lies within the range of 40–70 kg/kg, the specific heat consumption exceeds the specific heat of vaporization (r = 2360 kJ/kg) by a factor of 1.5, and the velocity of the air in the tube (natural draft) does not exceed 1.5 m/s. In the case of significant deviations from these values, their cause should be explained.
