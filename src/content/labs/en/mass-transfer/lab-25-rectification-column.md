# Laboratory Work №25. Studying the Operation of a Batch Packed Rectification Column

## Theoretical Background

In a batch rectification setup, which has only a concentrating section, the content of the light component x0 in the still continuously decreases, as a result of which the rectification process is unsteady over time. If the column of the setup operates at a constant reflux ratio R, the content of the light component in the distillate xD also continuously decreases. If it is necessary to maintain a constant concentration of the light component in the distillate (xD=const), the reflux ratio must be continuously increased during the rectification process.

The efficiency of a packed column can be expressed by the number of theoretical plates n or the number of transfer units m.

The number of theoretical plates in the column at any given time can be determined graphically, by drawing steps between the phase equilibrium line and the operating line within the range from x0 to xD (fig. 25.1).

The equation of the operating line

<div class="lab-formula"><img class="lab-formula-img" src="/images/labs/mass-transfer/lab-25/image1.png" alt="formula" /> or y =Ax+B,			(25.1)</div>

where A=R/(R+1) is the tangent of the angle of inclination to the abscissa axis; B= xD/(R+1) is the segment that the operating line cuts off from the origin on the ordinate axis.

During operation of the column at R=const, the value of A does not change, while the value of B continuously decreases, because as x0 decreases, xD also decreases. In this case, the operating line moves parallel to itself, approaching the abscissa axis.

During operation of the column at xD=const, the operating line, as x0 decreases, rotates counterclockwise around point a, since the point of its intersection with the diagonal does not shift (xD=const), while the value of B continuously decreases due to the increase of the reflux ratio R.

## Laboratory Setup

The packed column (fig. 25.4) consists of still section 1, which has heating circulation loop 3, sight glass 2, and thermometer 4. Above the still, through grate 5, column 6 (diameter 30 mm) with packing 7 (Raschig rings) is installed. The height of the packing layer is 800 mm. In the upper part of the column, thermometer 8 is installed. The column is connected to distillate collector 11 through dephlegmator 9. To regulate the process of changing the reflux ratio, valve 10 and reflux drop counter 12 are installed in the distillate withdrawal circuit.

Packing characteristics: Raschig rings 6×8×1 mm; specific surface area s = 735 m²/m³; free volume W = 0.5 m³/m³.

## Procedure

The ethanol–water mixture is subject to rectification. Before starting the work, one must check for the presence of the initial mixture in the still. Then we turn on the electric heater and monitor the readings of thermometer 4. As soon as the temperature in the still rises to 75°C, we open the water supply to dephlegmator 9; in this case, distillate withdrawal valve 10 should be closed.

<div class="lab-formula"><img class="lab-formula-img" src="/images/labs/mass-transfer/lab-25/image13.png" alt="formula" /> After the column has warmed up, alcohol-water vapor will begin to enter the dephlegmator. In this case, all the condensate will go to irrigating the column (the column operates "at total reflux," R = ). As soon as the temperature in the still and in the upper part of the column stabilizes (after 15–20 minutes), which means that the process in the column has stabilized, we record in table 25.1 the temperatures shown by the lower and upper thermometers.</div>

Then we set the reflux ratio. For this purpose, using valve 10, we set the required ratio of reflux and distillate flows (R should be within the range 1–5). After 15–20 minutes, the process stabilizes at the specified regime. One must record the thermometer readings and the pressure in the still. We repeat the measurements and recording 2–3 times.
