# Laboratory Work №10. Filtration of a Suspension on a Frame Filter Press

## Theoretical Background

Filtration is the process of separating non-uniform systems with a solid disperse phase through porous filter partitions that allow the dispersion medium (liquid or gas) to pass through while retaining the solid particles. Such separation, in particular of suspensions, is possible only when there is a pressure difference p, Pa, before and after the filter partition — the driving force of the process.

## Procedure

We prepare a suspension of water, perlite, and chalk so that the solid phase content in it is 15–20%. If the suspension has been prepared beforehand and is contained in reservoir 11, then before starting the experiment it is thoroughly mixed for 3–5 minutes using the pump, as described in the setup diagram.

Simultaneously with the preparation (mixing) of the suspension, we prepare the filter press for operation. The frames and plates must be well washed, and the filter cloth (individual sheets) moistened with water. When fitting the cloth sheets between the plates and frames, we ensure that the openings in their bosses coincide with the openings in the cloth sheets. After this, we tightly clamp the plates and frames together. The time spent assembling the filter τasm is recorded to determine the duration of auxiliary operations τaux.

We distribute duties among the participants. The first, using a stopwatch, tracks the time and at the set time τ (after 10, 20, 30, 60 s, etc.) gives the command "measurement." The second watches the scale readings and, at the moment the command is given, records the corresponding amount of filtrate from the start of the experiment. The third, using valve 5, maintains the required constant filtration pressure (driving force Δp) and records it.

Having completed all preparatory work, we begin testing the filter under a specified gauge pressure within the range of 0.05–0.1 MPa. We turn on electronic scale 10, close valve 9, partially close valve 12 to maintain circulatory mixing of the suspension, and open valve 2. We turn on pump 1 and use valve 5 to set the required pressure Δp. As soon as filtrate begins to flow from the plate taps, we start the stopwatch and measure the amount of filtrate at previously set times, i.e., at certain time intervals.

We continue the experiment until filtrate from the plate taps begins to flow into receiver 7 in a thin trickle. This means that the cavities (chambers) of the frames are almost completely filled with cake. We record the results of the last measurements of filtrate amount and time, close valve 5, and turn off the pump.

We disassemble the filter press, examine the cake formed on the cloth, and measure its thickness S for the subsequent calculation of V0. Then we wash the frames and cloth. The disassembly time τdis is added to τasm and we obtain τaux — the time for auxiliary operations during the operation of the batch filter.
