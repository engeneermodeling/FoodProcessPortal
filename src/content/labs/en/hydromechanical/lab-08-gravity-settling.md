# Laboratory Work №8. Settling in a Gravitational Field

## Theoretical Background

Non-uniform liquid systems with a more or less coarse dispersion of the disperse phase are separated solely under the action of gravity. The separation of particles from the viscous medium in which they are suspended, under the action of gravity, is called sedimentation, or settling.

The calculated quantity used in determining the dimensions and productivity of settling apparatuses is the settling velocity.

The theoretical settling velocity is determined from the general law of resistance to the motion of a body in a medium

G – A = R, 	(8.1)

where µ is the dynamic viscosity coefficient of the liquid phase, Pa·s.

## Laboratory Setup

The experimental setup for studying the settling process (see fig. 8.1) consists of two transparent settling cylinders 1, filled with liquid. Marks 2 are placed on the cylinders to register the beginning and end of settling. The diameter of the cylinder may vary, but must be such that surface tension forces do not affect the process of particle settling. It is desirable to choose a comparatively large height for the cylinders — this makes the experiment more accurate. Viscous liquids (sunflower or cottonseed oil) should be used for the experiments, because at low liquid viscosity it is difficult to register the duration of the experiment.

## Procedure

1. Pour liquid into the settling cylinders to a level higher than the upper mark.

2. Measure the temperature of the liquid.

3. Select spheres of different material and different diameter, determine the diameters with a caliper, and weigh the spheres on an analytical balance.

4. Calculate the density of the particle material.

5. Drop a sphere into the cylinder and determine the settling duration, i.e., the time it takes for the sphere to travel through the cylinder from the upper mark to the lower one.

For each type of sphere and for each liquid, repeat the experiment several times. Record the observation data and calculated quantities in the table.

## Processing the Results

1. Determine the density and viscosity of the liquid from a reference source, depending on the temperature.

2. Calculate the density of the sphere material, kg/m³,

ρ2 = m · 6/πd3,

where m is the mass of the sphere, kg; d is the diameter of the sphere, m.

3. Determine the actual settling velocity, m/s,

wact = H/τ,

where H is the distance between the marks on the cylinder, m; τ is the settling duration, s.

4. Calculate the Archimedes criterion using equation (8.4).

5. Calculate the Reynolds criterion.

6. Determine the theoretical settling velocity.
