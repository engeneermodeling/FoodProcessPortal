## Procedure

The settling process in a thin-layer settler is modeled by settling in a "vertical cut-out" of the channel, for example, in a cylinder (fig. 9.2), whose height must be no less than h (h = H/cosα), and whose diameter should be as large as possible (no less than 50 mm) to avoid the influence of the walls on the settling process.

<div class="lab-figure">
  <img src="/images/labs/hydromechanical/lab-09/image5.png" alt="Figure for laboratory work №9" loading="lazy" />
</div>

We pour suspension into the cylinder, and at that same moment begin to record the change over time of the position hset — the interface surface between the clarified liquid and the sediment. We display the settling process graphically (see fig. 9.2).

1. We select the channel height H and the angle of its inclination to the horizontal α.

2. We experimentally obtain the relationship between the desired division of the suspension into clarified liquid and sediment and the duration of the process (see fig. 9.2).

3. We calculate wl max and wl min and select wl.

4. The value of τset, selected with the help of fig. 9.2, is used to calculate L and LΣ (equations (9.5) and (9.6)).

5. We select the channel width b.

6. Using equation (9.7), we determine n, if V is given, or V, if n is given.

In the case where the relationship between L, b, and n is not convenient, we change wl or b and repeat the calculation.
