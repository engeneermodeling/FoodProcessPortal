# Laboratory Work №5. Experimental Determination of the Coefficients of Liquid Discharge Through Orifices and Nozzles

## Theoretical Background

Orifices and nozzles are used for liquid discharge. Liquid can flow out of orifices at constant or variable pressure into the atmosphere or below the liquid level (unsubmerged or submerged orifice).

Orifices are divided, by their relative size, into small and large, and by relative wall thickness — into orifices in thin and thick walls. An orifice is considered small if the pressure change over its height can be neglected, and its vertical dimension z ≤ 0.1h (d ≤ 0.1h). An orifice is considered large if the vertical dimension of its live cross-section is z ≥ 0.1h (d ≥ 0.1h), where h is the head above the center of gravity of the orifice.

In the case of liquid discharge from an external cylindrical nozzle (fig. 5.2), the jet first contracts as it enters the nozzle (dc ≈ 0.8d), then gradually expands and fills the entire cross-section of the nozzle.

In this case, the velocity coefficient φ at the outlet equals the discharge coefficient µ, because there is no contraction of the jet at the exit from the nozzle (ε = 1).

Inside the nozzle, at the point of jet contraction, an annular "dead" space is created, from which air is carried away by the flow, as a result of which a vacuum forms in this zone, whose value depends on the flow velocity or head. The low pressure in the contracted cross-section causes additional suction of liquid and, consequently, an increase in its discharge rate. The vacuum in the nozzle

pvac/(ρg) = 0.75h.

The water flowing out of the orifice enters reservoir 5, mounted on scale 7. The head is monitored with piezometer 8. The actual discharge velocity is determined with coordinate meter 4.

## Procedure

1. After filling vessel 1 with water, close air valve 9.

2. Open the orifice in the thin wall.

3. After establishing a constant liquid discharge velocity, determine the head h using piezometer 8, and measure the diameter of the contracted jet dc with a caliper.

4. Close valve 6 and balance reservoir 5, together with the water entering it, on scale 7 using the necessary weights. At the moment of reaching balance, start the stopwatch, after which weights are placed on the scale pan, whose mass depends on the amount of water that needs to pass through the orifice. At the moment of the next balance, stop the stopwatch. During the experiment, measure the coordinates X and Y of an arbitrary point of the jet using coordinate meter 4.

5. Open the orifice with the external cylindrical nozzle and repeat the experiment according to steps 1–4.
