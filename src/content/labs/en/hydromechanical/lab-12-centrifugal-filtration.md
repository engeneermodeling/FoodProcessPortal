# Laboratory Work №12. Centrifugal Filtration

Centrifugal filtration is the process of separating suspensions in a centrifugal force field using liquid-permeable partitions. The final goal of this process is to obtain a cake with a minimal content of liquid phase, or a filtrate with a minimal content of solid phase.

Energy consumption for a batch centrifuge is calculated separately for the start-up and operating periods. The maximum energy consumption is required during the start-up period, when the inertia of the mass of the centrifuge's rotating parts and the loaded material is added. ## Laboratory Setup

The main element of the setup with the experimental centrifuge (see fig. 12.1) is a batch filtering centrifuge, consisting of perforated drum 2, fastened to vertical shaft 4, which is connected by a coupling to electric motor 5. The centrifuge shaft is connected to a tachometer for measuring the rotational speed of the drum.

Housing 1 of the centrifuge is closed by cover 3. In the bottom of the housing there is a nozzle for discharging the filtrate into measuring cylinder 6. The rotational speed of the centrifuge drum is regulated using a laboratory autotransformer.

In this work, suspension filtration is modeled by the process of removing water from a moistened foam rubber strip.

## Procedure

1. We determine the dimensions of the centrifuge drum and its mass.

2. We close the cover of the centrifuge housing.

3. We turn on the centrifuge's electric motor by turning the laboratory autotransformer's knob to the position corresponding to the drum rotational speed set by the instructor, and use a stopwatch to determine the duration of the centrifuge's acceleration to the specified rotational speed.

4. We turn off the electric motor and, after the centrifuge has come to a complete stop, open the housing cover.

5. We moisten the foam rubber strip in water or another liquid and place it uniformly inside the centrifuge drum.

6. We close the housing cover and turn on the electric motor by turning the laboratory autotransformer's knob to the position corresponding to the specified rotational speed of the centrifuge drum.

7. We determine the duration of the centrifuge's acceleration and the mass of the filtrate.

8. After filtration is finished, we turn off the electric motor.

9. After the centrifuge drum has come to a complete stop, we open the housing cover and remove the foam rubber strip.

10. We determine the masses of the foam rubber strip and the filtrate.

11. We perform the necessary calculations and prepare the experiment protocol.

## Processing the Results

After finishing the work, prepare a protocol that should contain: a diagram of the setup and the centrifuge drum indicating the main dimensions; a brief description of the setup and its operating principle; the measurement results, calculations of the driving force, separation factor, energy consumption during the start-up and operating periods, an analysis of the research results, and conclusions.

## Review Questions

1. What are the main elements of the experimental setup, and what is their purpose?
2. How does a filtering centrifuge work, and what is its difference from a settling centrifuge?
3. What components make up the energy consumption of a batch centrifuge, and how are they determined?
4. For what purpose, and how, is the centrifuge's start-up duration determined?
5. Why, when selecting a motor for a centrifuge, is its power increased compared to the calculated value for the start-up period?
6. How is the driving force determined? How does it affect the process of centrifugal filtration and settling?
7. What is the separation factor? How is it determined? What are the ways to increase it?
8. What are the features of determining the filtration rate under the action of centrifugal force?
9. What serves as the basis for comparing centrifuges?
10. How does the rotational speed of the centrifuge drum affect the speed and quality of separation in filtering and settling centrifuges?
