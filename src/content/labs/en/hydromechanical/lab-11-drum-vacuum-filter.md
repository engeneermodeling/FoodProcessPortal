# Laboratory Work №11. Filtration of a Suspension on a Drum Vacuum Filter

## Theoretical Background

The filtration equations for compressible and incompressible cakes, used in the calculations of filter presses, cannot be used for calculating continuously operating drum vacuum filters, since the conditions of cake formation in them are different.

The thickness of the cake layer on the drum surface submerged in the suspension changes from 0 to h0. Accordingly, the filtration rate also changes, from the initial wv, determined during the filtration of pure water through the filter partition, to the final one, determined during the filtration of suspension through a cake layer of thickness h0.

## Laboratory Setup

The experimental vacuum filtration setup (fig. 11.1) is mounted on a frame framework. On it is also installed a panel with control, monitoring, and measuring instruments.

The main working element of the setup is a horizontal drum of cylindrical shape with a perforated surface (fig. 11.2). The drum surface is divided by longitudinal partitions into twelve sections (chambers), connected by tubes through channels in the trunnion to movable disc 1, which rotates together with the drum, being in continuous contact with stationary disc 2 (fig. 11.3). The contact planes of the movable and stationary discs are finished to a high degree of precision and lapped to each other, which ensures the tightness of their connection in the moving state. Assembled in a single housing, the movable and stationary discs form a distribution device.

The stationary disc of the distribution device has four chambers. The first (lower) chamber is connected to filtrate collector 31, the second — to wash water collector 26, the third — to compressor 47 for compressed air used to blow off the cake from the cloth before it is cut off by the knife, the fourth — to the supply line of the regenerating agent (compressed air) for regenerating the filter cloth. Compressed air is supplied to the last two chambers from compressor 47 through receiver (accumulator) 1.

The cylindrical surface of the drum is covered on the outside with filter cloth. The drum is rotated through a gearbox by a DC electric motor, which allows the rotational speed of the drum to be varied from 0.01 to 0.05 s⁻¹.

One third of the drum's working surface is submerged in vessel 35, whose lower part is equipped with agitator 36 to prevent stratification of the suspension. The rotational speed of the agitator is 3.3 s⁻¹.

During rotation of the drum, each chamber sequentially passes through the zones of filtration, drying, cake washing, cake blowing, and filter cloth regeneration, connecting in turn through the movable disc with the corresponding chambers of the stationary disc of the distribution device.

On the side surface of vessel 35, knife 40 for cutting off the cake is fastened on one side, and overflow device 32 for returning excess suspension to tank 19 is fastened on the opposite side. The cut-off cake falls into chute 39 and then enters collector 38.

Tank 19, for preparing the suspension, heating it, and storing it, is equipped with agitator 18 and a water jacket, in which the water is heated by electric heating element 17 and, through the housing walls, raises the temperature of the suspension. To create circulation of the heating water in the tank's jacket, valve 11 is used. Agitator 18 is used to stir up the suspension in order to prevent cake formation and to speed up heating. The suspension from tank 19 is fed into working vessel 35 of the filter by centrifugal pump 12, built into the tank.

The setup's design provides for filtrate collector 31 and wash water collector 26, equipped with measuring glass tubes 23 and 27, vacuum gauges 30 and 25, and the corresponding regulating fittings.

To create a vacuum in the system, the filtrate and wash water collectors are connected to vacuum pump 5 through condenser 22 with condensate collector 16. Between the condenser and the vacuum pump, drop separator 7 is installed. Separator 4 is intended for capturing oil droplets carried out by the air from the vacuum pump.

Valves 3, 6, 8–10, 13–15 are drain valves. Valve 28 connects the filtrate and wash water collectors to the atmosphere in order to neutralize the vacuum after the setup finishes operating.

From the sections of the drum submerged in the suspension, filtrate is drawn off to filtrate collector 31, and cake forms and accumulates on the outer surface of the filter cloth.

The cake is washed with water supplied through sprinkler 34, and the wash water is drawn off to collector 26. To facilitate cutting the washed cake off the filter cloth, it is blown with compressed air, which is supplied from receiver 1 through a pipeline with valves 2 and 45. Valve 46 regulates the supply of compressed air for regenerating the filter cloth. Air pressure is regulated according to the readings of pressure gauges 43 and 44.

After the setup is stopped, the spent liquids are drained into the sewer.

## Procedure

EXPERIMENT ON WATER

Before starting the work, we inspect the setup for readiness and make sure that all taps and valves are closed. After this, we turn on the main power switch of the setup.

We open valve 20 and fill tank 19 with water. We turn on agitator 18 and electric heater 17.

We turn on pump 12, open valves 15, 13, and 37, and fill the filter's vessel with water, regulating the water supply with valve 37 so that the excess flows freely into overflow device 32, and from there — into tank 19.

We turn on the electric motor driving the filter drum and measure the rotational speed of the drum.

After the filter cloth has become moistened, we turn on the vacuum pump and use valve 29 to set the specified value of vacuum in filtrate collector 31. We open valve 21 for supplying tap water into the intertubular space of the condenser.

We measure the time and the volume of water that enters filtrate collector 31 during the experiment, and also record the water temperature and the vacuum in it.

Having finished the experiment, we close valve 20, valves 15, 13, and 37, and turn off pump 12, vacuum pump 5, heater 17, agitator 18, and the drum drive.

If the experiment on water is not carried out, the resistance coefficient of the filter partition can be assumed equal to R0 = 5.3 · 10¹¹ m⁻¹.

EXPERIMENT ON SUSPENSION

Into suspension tank 19, filled with water, with agitator 18 running, we pour perlite powder in such an amount that the solid phase content in the suspension is 10–15% (mass). We heat the suspension to a temperature of 40–50°C.

We turn on pump 12, open valves 15, 13, and 37, and fill the filter's vessel with suspension, regulating with valve 37 the free draining of excess suspension into tank 19.

After the filter cloth has become moistened (if it is dry), we turn on the vacuum pump and use valve 29 to set the specified vacuum in filtrate collector 31. We carry out the work without washing the cake, so the wash water collector is not connected to the vacuum pump.

We turn on the compressor, open valve 2, and use valves 45 and 46 to set an air pressure of 0.02–0.03 MPa for blowing off the cake and regenerating the filter cloth.

After the drum has made more than one revolution and the knife begins to cut off the cake (the operating mode has stabilized), we measure the duration of the experiment, the volume of filtrate, the temperature of the suspension, the vacuum in the filtrate collector, the thickness of the cake, and the rotational speed of the drum.

To illustrate cake washing, we open valve 24, connect wash water collector 26 to the vacuum system, and then use valve 33 to supply water into sprinkler 34.

We shut off the wash water supply (valve 33), close all valves and taps, and turn off the vacuum pump, centrifugal pump, compressor, agitator, suspension heater, filter drum drive, and its agitator.

## Review Questions

1. Name the main structural elements of a drum vacuum filtration setup and state their purpose.
2. Which physical quantities in the Redko equation are filtration constants, and which can be considered adjustable process parameters?
3. Analyze how and which physical quantities can be changed to increase the filtration rate.
4. Why heat the suspension during filtration, and how can this be explained using the Redko equation?
5. What value can the driving force of filtration on a drum vacuum filter not exceed? Where is the driving force greater: in the lower zone of the drum, or in the zone near the free surface of the suspension?
6. For what purpose is the cake washed and dried? Is this necessary to do when conducting the experiment?
7. By what method and for what purpose is the filter cloth regenerated?
