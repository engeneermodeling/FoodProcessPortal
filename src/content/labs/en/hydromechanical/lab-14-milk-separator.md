# Laboratory Work №14. Studying the Operation of a Centrifugal Milk Separator

## Theoretical Background

The separator is intended for separating whole milk into cream and skim milk (skimmed whey). Milk fat is contained in milk in the form of tiny globules with a diameter of 1–10 µm. Since the density difference between skim milk and fat is small (1030.6 and 911.9 kg/m³ at a temperature of 30°C) and the size of the fat globules is small, under gravitational settling conditions they rise too slowly. Separators are used to significantly accelerate this process.

During the separator's operation, milk is supplied in a continuous stream into the rapidly rotating drum. As the milk moves through the drum, cream moves toward the drum's axis of rotation and enters the cream receiver through a special adjustable opening. Skim milk, having a higher density, is thrown by centrifugal force from the inter-plate space toward the periphery, and moving above the distribution plate, enters the receiver for skim milk. Mechanical impurities are thrown against the drum walls and settle on them in a dense layer. In the inter-plate gap, the fat globules move together with the milk flow downward toward the periphery of the plates at the flow velocity, m/s,

where ω is the angular velocity of the drum's rotation, 1/s; R is the radius of rotation, m; d is the diameter of the fat globule, m; ρsm, ρf are, respectively, the density of skim milk and of fat, kg/m³; µ is the dynamic viscosity coefficient of skim milk, Pa·s.

The density of skim milk ρsm, the density of fat ρf, and the viscosity of milk µ depend on temperature and can be determined from the equation (for temperatures from 15 to 75°C)

(ρsm – ρf)/µ = 2900t,			(14.3)

where t is the temperature of the milk, °C.

Taking this equation into account, the settling velocity of fat globules can be determined from a simpler relationship

w0 = 1612Rd²t,  or  w0 = 6400n²R d²t,

where n is the rotational speed of the drum, 1/s.

During gravitational settling under the acceleration of gravity g, the settling velocity will be considerably smaller and is determined by the equation

w0 = 161 g d²t = 1580 d²t.	(14.4)

The absolute velocity of the fat globule's motion equals the geometric sum of the two velocities wf and w0. As the fat globule moves away from the axis of rotation, the velocity w0 increases while the flow velocity wf decreases, because the flow's cross-sectional area increases as R increases. As a result, the direction of the fat globule's absolute velocity, as it approaches the periphery, will turn back toward the axis of rotation.

The productivity of the separator, m³/h,

V = 48 · 10⁶  β n² z tg α (R³l – R³s),					    (14.5)

where β is the technological efficiency of the separator (the degree of utilization of the drum's working volume, part of which is occupied by cream), β = 0.5...0.7; α is the angle of inclination of the plate (from 45 to 60°); Rl, Rs are the large and small radii of the plate, respectively, m.

The temperature of the milk during separation should be from 36 to 40°C. The calculated diameter of the fat globule equals 1.3–1.5 µm. In this case, the fat content in the skim milk will be 0.04–0.06%.

The density of milk is determined with a milk hydrometer at a temperature of 15–25°C. If the temperature of the milk at the time of determination is higher or lower than 20°C, the measurement results are reduced to 20°C using the corresponding table.

## Laboratory Setup

The working element of the separator (fig. 14.1) is drum 4, which consists of plate holder 7 with a set of aluminum plates 5, distribution plate 3, and chambers 1 and 2 for discharging the light and heavy fractions.

The distance between the plates, which equals approximately 0.5 mm, is fixed by projections on the surface of the plates. The plates have openings that form channels 6 for supplying milk into the inter-plate space. In the gaps between the plates, the milk is separated into a light fraction (cream) and skim milk (whey). The cream is displaced by the heavier fraction toward the axis of rotation and moves toward the cream discharge chamber. The whey is thrown by centrifugal force toward the periphery of the drum and, under the pressure of fresh milk, moves above the distribution plate toward the discharge chamber.

## Procedure

1. We place the assembled drum onto the conical part of the shaft, pressing it slightly with the hand from above.

2. We install the receiver for skim milk and the receiver for cream. In doing so, one must pay attention to the correctness of their installation on the housing and relative to each other.

3. We rotate the drum by hand using the nut, to make sure it does not touch the housing or the whey and cream receivers.

4. We set the spouts of the whey and cream receivers to a convenient position, and place containers under them for whey (larger) and for cream (smaller).

5. We install the float chamber, insert the float, install the milk receiver, insert the plastic plug into the conical opening of the housing at the bottom of the milk receiver. The plug in the milk receiver should be in the "Closed" position, i.e., the pointed part of the plug's handle turned away from the notch on the edge of the milk receiver.

6. Before connecting the separator to the electrical mains, we set the toggle switch to position "0", i.e., off. After this, we insert the cord's plug into the outlet.

7. We pour filtered whole milk into the tank with heater and agitator, turn on the heater and agitator, and use the thermostat to heat the milk to a temperature of 35–40°C.

8. We open the valve for supplying milk from the tank to the pump, turn on the pump, and pump the milk into the bowl of the milk receiver.

9. We turn on the separator's electric drive. After the drum has reached full rotational speed (30–40 s after turning on), we open the valve, i.e., turn the plug's handle (with the pointed part) toward the notch on the edge of the milk receiver.

10. After finishing separation, we pour approximately 3 L of warm water into the milk bowl for rinsing, and pass it through the running separator to remove residues of whey and cream.

11. We turn off the electric motor.

12. We carefully remove the drum from the electric drive's shaft. Excessive rocking of the drum on the shaft is not allowed, as it can lead to deformation of the shaft or the shaft's cone.

13. We disassemble the drum using a special wrench. We unscrew the nut. To remove the drum's cover, which usually fits tightly on the plate holder, we turn the drum over and strike the end of the threaded part against a wooden board. It is prohibited to strike the drum's threaded end against a hard surface.

14. Having opened the drum, we remove the distribution plate, then remove the pack of plates and the rubber sealing ring. When disassembling the drum, the adjusting screw in the upper part of the distribution plate should not be unscrewed.

15. We assemble the drum in the reverse order of disassembly. First we place the rubber ring in the groove. Then, on the plate holder, we install a type "A" plate (with an opening symmetrical to the projections), followed by a type "B" plate (with the opening offset), and so on, alternately installing plates of the two types.

16. We cover the pack of plates from above with the distribution plate, so that its projection enters the groove of the drum's cover.

17. We align the "0" mark on the drum's cover with the same mark on the lower part of the plate holder.

18. We screw on the nut by hand. We finally tighten the nut with a special wrench, inserting its two prongs into the two openings of the nut.

We tighten the nut until the "0" mark on the nut aligns with the notch on the threaded part of the plate holder.

19. The drum parts should be washed with warm water after each separation. We remove milk and dirt residues with a brush, and clean all channels with a small brush.

20. If needed, we regulate the density of the cream with the screw with a square opening, installed in the upper part of the distribution plate. To increase the fat content in the cream, the screw should be turned clockwise; to decrease it — counterclockwise. Usually one turn in one direction or the other is sufficient for the required adjustment.

21. We determine the amount of separation products (cream and skim milk) in liters, the ratio between the volumes of cream and whey, and also the productivity of the separator using measuring cylinders.

22. We determine the density of the cream and skim milk.

23. We determine the separation factor

Fr = 4π²n²Ravg,

where Ravg is the average radius of rotation, m; Ravg = (Rl + Rs)/2; n is the rotational speed of the drum, s⁻¹; n = 10,500 1/min.

24. We determine the productivity of the separator using equation (14.5).

25. We compare the calculated productivity with that actually established during the experiment, and with the rated (nameplate) milk productivity, which should be no less than 80 L/h.

## Processing the Results

After finishing the work, prepare a protocol in which the design and operating principle of the separator are described; drawings of the two types of working plates are made, with the dimensions of the large and small radii of rotation and the angle of inclination; all calculated quantities relating to the testing of the separator are recorded, and conclusions are drawn.

4. Why does cream move toward the center of the drum, while skim milk moves toward the periphery?
