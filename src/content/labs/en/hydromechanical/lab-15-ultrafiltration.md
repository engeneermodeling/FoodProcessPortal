# Laboratory Work №15. Separation and Concentration of Solutions on an Ultrafiltration Setup

## Theoretical Background

Membrane processes are used for separating (concentrating) non-uniform systems with very fine particles, whose behavior is determined by intermolecular interaction forces and which participate in Brownian motion. In this case, the membranes themselves have openings comparable in size to the particles, which are partly or fully characterized by the interaction forces of the membrane material's molecules. Due to the influence of molecular forces, unlike ordinary filtration, the driving force of membrane processes must be considerably greater, no cake forms on the surface of the membranes, and the result of separation is not a cake and a pure liquid — filtrate — but a diluted and a more concentrated product.

Depending on the size (diameter) d of the pores and the operating pressure of the process ΔP, baromembrane processes can be rather conditionally divided into the following:

microfiltration (d = 0.1…10 µm, ΔP = 0.2 MPa);

ultrafiltration (d = 0.01…0.1 µm, ΔP = 0.2…1.0 MPa);

reverse osmosis (d ≤ 0.01 µm, ΔP = 1…10 MPa).

Unlike reverse osmosis, ultrafiltration is applied for separating systems in which the molecular mass of the dissolved components is considerably greater than the molecular mass of the solvent. In the food industry, ultrafiltration is used for purifying and concentrating fruit and vegetable juices, preparing technological process water, extracting protein from potato juice and milk whey, separating animal blood, isolating enzymes, and so on.

The driving force of the ultrafiltration separation process is the pressure difference before and after the membrane. Under the action of this pressure difference, the membrane passes some components and retains others.

The accumulation of sediment particles of the dissolved substance near the membrane surface is unacceptable, since it leads to its clogging. To avoid an increase in the concentration of dissolved substance near the membrane surface (concentration polarization) due to the transfer of solvent through the membrane, the liquid layer adjacent to the membrane surface must be turbulized. This accelerates the transfer of the dissolved substance into the main volume of the solution being separated. In membrane separation of gases, concentration polarization is very small.

Two indicators of the ultrafiltration process are of the greatest practical interest: productivity and selectivity.

The specific productivity (permeability) G of the membrane separation process at a given pressure is determined by the formula

G = V/Fτ,						 (15.1)

where V is the volume of the final product; F is the working surface area of the membrane; τ is the duration of the process.

Permeability G is usually expressed in liters per square meter per hour, or in kilograms per square meter per second.

## Laboratory Setup

The ultrafiltration setup (fig. 15.1) consists of membrane module 1, centrifugal-type circulation pump 2 with a 1.5 kW electric motor, heat exchanger 3, shut-off and control fittings 4, pressure gauges 5, and a special circulation tank 6 with a volume of 0.025 m³. The elements of the setup that come into contact with the process medium are made of stainless steel and chemically thermostable materials.

The setup is intended for the ultrafiltration of biological suspensions and solutions in tubular-type apparatuses. One can study membrane separation in tubular elements while working out the technological modes of processes with various membranes (polysulfone, fluoroplastic, polysulfonamide), and also process small batches of liquid product (up to 30 L). The setup can operate with individual tubular elements, with shortened blocks of tubular elements (1 m long), and with industrial blocks (2 m long, membrane area — 0.5 m²).

## Procedure

Before starting the setup, we determine the productivity of the tubular elements with distilled water, for which we fill reservoir 6 with distilled water with valves 4a and 4b open, in order to completely fill the setup.

We connect heat exchanger 3 to a source of cold water (if required by the technological process).

We start the setup with valves 4a, 4c, 4d closed and valve 4b fully open, using a magnetic starter from a 380 V power source. We conduct the study of productivity with water until an unchanging value of V1 is reached, which indicates a stable membrane permeability. During this, the membrane swells.

We prepare the sucrose aqueous solution under study according to the instructor's assignment. We pour the solution into the tank for membrane separation.

Using valves 4a and 4b, we set the pressure difference in the separation module specified by the instructor.

We start the stopwatch when the first drops appear at the outlet. We periodically record the time as the amount of obtained product V2 changes (100, 200, 300, ..., 900, 1000 mL).

In the obtained portions of the final product, we determine the dry substance content (amount of sucrose) using a laboratory refractometer.

After completing the laboratory work, we regenerate the membranes of the ultrafiltration setup by rinsing with distilled water.
