# Lecture 22. Sieving, Classification and Sorting of Food Materials

## Learning Outcomes

After this lecture you will be able to distinguish sieving, classification and sorting, define sieve oversize and undersize, identify factors affecting screening efficiency, and place aspiration, magnets and optical sorting in a food processing line.

---

## 1. Purpose

After milling, drying, granulation or raw-material reception, food materials are rarely uniform. They may contain coarse particles, dust, lumps, husks, stones, metal fragments, damaged grains or off-colour particles.

Separation is used to obtain the target size fraction, remove impurities, stabilize quality, protect downstream machines, improve food safety and return oversize material for regrinding.

---

## 2. Sieving

Sieving separates by geometric particle size. The material passing through the openings is the undersize. The material retained on the screen is the oversize.

![Sieve fraction flow](/images/lectures/mechanical/sieve-fraction-flow.svg)

Real screening is affected by particle shape, moisture, stickiness, bed depth, blinding of apertures and contact time.

---

## 3. Screening Efficiency

Efficiency depends on more than the aperture size.

![Screening efficiency factors](/images/lectures/mechanical/sieve-efficiency.svg)

Important factors include feed rate, bed depth, moisture, particle shape, vibration amplitude and frequency, screen inclination, aperture cleanliness and electrostatic or fatty adhesion.

---

## 4. Air Classification, Magnetic and Optical Sorting

Aspiration separates dust, husks and light impurities by aerodynamic behaviour. Magnetic separators remove ferromagnetic contaminants. Optical sorters use cameras, lighting and air jets to remove defects by colour, shape, size or spectral response.

![Food sorting line](/images/lectures/mechanical/food-sorting-line.svg)

Optical sorting is useful for grains, rice, nuts, coffee, spices, pulses, dried fruit and frozen vegetables.

---

## 5. Typical Line

A raw-material cleaning line may include sampling, coarse screening, aspiration, magnets, size calibration, optical sorting and final fraction quality control. The exact order depends on the material and risk profile.
