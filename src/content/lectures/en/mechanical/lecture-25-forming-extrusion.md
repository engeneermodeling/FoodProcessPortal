# Lecture 25. Forming and Extrusion of Food Masses

## Learning Outcomes

After this lecture you will be able to explain die forming, distinguish cold and cooking extrusion, describe extruder zones and identify variables controlling shape, expansion, texture and drying stability.

---

## 1. Forming

Forming gives a food mass its geometry and structure. Sometimes it mainly shapes the product, as in pasta dough. In other cases it combines mixing, heating, shear, starch transformation, protein structuring and expansion.

This lecture focuses on food engineering, not biotechnology or fermentation processes.

---

## 2. Extrusion

Extrusion forces a plastic or viscous mass through a die. A food extruder can convey, mix, compress, heat, shear and shape the product in one continuous unit.

![Extruder zones](/images/lectures/mechanical/extruder-zones.svg)

Main parts include feed system, screw or twin screws, barrel temperature zones, compression and shear zones, die, cutter and downstream drying or cooling.

---

## 3. Process Window

Stable products require a controlled process window. Too little water overloads the screw and gives poor forming. Too much water causes stickiness and weak shape. Too much heat may cause darkening, burning or off-flavours.

![Extrusion process window](/images/lectures/mechanical/extrusion-process-window.svg)

Key variables are moisture, barrel temperature, screw speed, screw configuration, die pressure, die geometry, cutting speed and post-extrusion drying or cooling.

---

## 4. Products

![Extruded food products](/images/lectures/mechanical/extruded-food-products.svg)

Pasta uses cold forming and drying to set structure. Snacks and breakfast cereals use thermomechanical treatment and expansion. Breadcrumbs use extrusion, drying and grinding. Textured plant products use moisture, heat, shear and cooling to create layered or fibrous structures.

---

## 5. Defects

Typical defects include cracks after drying, unstable shape, uneven expansion, stickiness, burnt particles, excessive hardness or brittleness, cutting waste and die blockage. Causes often combine formulation, moisture, die design, temperature and drying.
