# Lecture 24. Agglomeration, Granulation and Instantization of Food Powders

## Learning Outcomes

After this lecture you will be able to explain why fine powders are enlarged, distinguish agglomeration, granulation, pelleting and instantization, and identify the key variables controlling wettability, flowability and caking.

---

## 1. Why Make Particles Larger?

Fine food powders may be dusty, poorly wettable, difficult to dose, prone to caking or unstable during storage. Controlled enlargement can improve handling and consumer use.

Examples include instant dairy powders, cocoa drinks, coffee and tea powders, dry soups, sauces, protein blends, instant flours and pelleted by-products.

---

## 2. Mechanisms

Particles may join through liquid bridges, sticky surfaces, crystallization during drying, mechanical compaction, binders or partial melting of fats or sugars.

![Agglomeration mechanisms](/images/lectures/mechanical/agglomeration-bridges.svg)

Weak bonds create dust; overly strong or moisture-sensitive bonds may cause caking.

---

## 3. Instant Powders

Fine powders often float, lump and wet slowly. Porous agglomerates allow water to enter, sink faster and disperse more easily.

![Instant powder wetting](/images/lectures/mechanical/instant-powder-wetting.svg)

Important indicators are agglomerate size, porosity, bulk density, wettability, dispersibility, solubility, dust content and storage stability.

---

## 4. Granulation and Pelleting

Granulation uses moisture, binder and mechanical energy to create particles of a target size range. Pelleting compacts material through a die to create dense cylindrical pellets.

![Pelleting line](/images/lectures/mechanical/pelletizing-line.svg)

Critical variables include moisture, conditioning temperature, binder, pre-grind size, die channel, pressure, friction, cooling and removal of fines.

---

## 5. Caking

Caking is unwanted particle bonding during storage. It is driven by humidity, crystallization of sugars or salts, amorphous components, surface fat, pressure, temperature cycling and fines. Control requires moisture management, suitable packaging, particle-size control and storage conditions.
