# Lecture 27. Washing, Cleaning, Grading and Inspection of Food Raw Materials

## Learning Outcomes

After this lecture you will be able to explain the role of raw-material preparation, distinguish dry cleaning, wet washing, grading and inspection, select washing methods and identify wash-water and cross-contamination risks.

---

## 1. Purpose

Raw materials may contain soil, dust, leaves, stones, damaged pieces, insects, foreign grains, metal, sand and microbial contamination. Preparation stabilizes downstream processing and improves quality and safety.

![Raw material cleaning line](/images/lectures/mechanical/raw-material-cleaning-line.svg)

No single step guarantees safety. Reliable preparation combines reception, dry cleaning, washing, rinsing, dewatering, grading, inspection and correct handling after cleaning.

---

## 2. Dry Cleaning and Washing

Dry cleaning uses screens, aspiration, brushes, stone removers, magnets and optical sorting. It is especially important for grain, seeds, spices and materials where water would create storage or microbial problems.

Washing removes soil and surface contamination but does not sterilize raw materials.

![Washing methods](/images/lectures/mechanical/washing-methods.svg)

Method selection depends on product strength, shape, soil load, surface texture, bruising sensitivity, water removal and acceptable product loss.

---

## 3. Wash-Water Control

Wash water can clean or spread contamination.

![Wash-water control](/images/lectures/mechanical/wash-water-control.svg)

Important controls include turbidity, temperature, pH, organic load, sanitizer residual where used, contact time, water replacement or recirculation, filtration and condition of tanks, nozzles and conveyors.

---

## 4. Grading and Inspection

Grading separates by size, mass, shape or quality so that peeling, cutting, blanching, drying, freezing and packing are more uniform. Inspection may be manual or sensor-based. Optical sorting can detect colour defects, foreign materials, damaged kernels and quality defects in grains, fruit, vegetables, nuts and dried products.
