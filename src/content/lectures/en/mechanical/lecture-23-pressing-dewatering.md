# Lecture 23. Pressing and Mechanical Dewatering

## Learning Outcomes

After this lecture you will be able to explain how pressure expels liquid from a porous food material, describe screw and batch presses, identify factors affecting juice, oil or whey yield, and recognize quality risks such as heating, emulsification and poor drainage.

---

## 1. Principle

Pressing removes liquid mechanically by compressing a porous material. The material may be crushed fruit, oilseed flakes, curd, pomace, wet solids or paste.

![Pressing principle](/images/lectures/mechanical/pressing-principle.svg)

During compression the solid phase becomes denser while liquid flows through pores, screens, cloths or drainage channels. If the drainage path closes, more pressure gives little benefit and may damage quality.

---

## 2. Food Applications

Pressing is used for fruit and vegetable juices, oilseeds, curd dewatering, pomace moisture reduction, wet solids before drying and cake preparation.

![Food pressing examples](/images/lectures/mechanical/pressing-food-examples.svg)

---

## 3. Main Variables

Important variables include pressure, pressing time, temperature, particle size, bed permeability, pectin, protein, fat or starch content, pre-treatment, drainage design and bed thickness.

For fruits, excessive crushing may create a puree that drains poorly. For oilseeds, flaking and conditioning are crucial. For curd, pressure must be gradual enough to avoid sealing the surface.

---

## 4. Screw Press

A screw press operates continuously. The screw moves material through a narrowing chamber, pressure increases, liquid exits through a perforated cage and the compressed solid cake leaves at the discharge.

![Screw press scheme](/images/lectures/mechanical/screw-press-scheme.svg)

Advantages are continuous operation, high capacity and compactness. Limitations include heating, wear, emulsification risk and sensitivity to feed preparation.

---

## 5. Quality and Energy

Maximum yield is not always the best result. Excessive pressure may increase bitterness, turbidity, cell damage, oxidation or fine solids in the liquid. Mechanical water removal is usually cheaper than evaporation, so pressing is often used before drying, but bound moisture still requires thermal or combined methods.
