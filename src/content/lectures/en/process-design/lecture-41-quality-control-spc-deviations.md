# Lecture 41. Quality Control, SPC and Deviation Handling

## What You Will Learn

After this lecture you will be able to connect product quality with process parameters, choose control points on a food line, distinguish laboratory, online and sensory control, read a simple SPC chart and describe what to do when a deviation occurs.

---

## 1. Quality Must Be Controlled, Not Only Tested

Finished product testing is necessary, but it is late. If the problem is already in a packed batch, correction is expensive. Quality is built into the process: raw materials, dosing, temperature, time, mixing, size reduction, cooling, filling, packaging and storage.

A good control system answers three questions: what is measured, where it is measured and what decision follows from the result.

![Quality control loop](/images/lectures/process-design/quality-control-loop.svg)

---

## 2. Specifications and Critical Characteristics

A specification describes what the product or intermediate should be. In food manufacturing it may include dry matter or Brix, moisture, pH, acidity, fat, protein, salt, sugar, viscosity, texture, temperature, net weight, color, odor, taste, microbiology and absence of foreign bodies.

Not all characteristics are equally critical. Some affect safety, others stability, consumer quality or label compliance.

---

## 3. Control Points on the Line

Control points should be placed where the result can still be corrected or where a decision about product status is needed.

Examples include raw material receiving, after dosing, after heat treatment, after cooling, before filling, after filling and after CIP. For a course project, it is enough to show five to eight key points with parameter, method and decision.

---

## 4. Laboratory, Online and Sensory Control

Laboratory control is accurate but often slow. Online sensors are fast but need calibration and verification. Sensory control is important for consumer quality, but it must be standardized: sample, conditions, scale and trained assessors.

Engineers combine these methods. A pH sensor may provide continuous data, the laboratory periodically confirms it, and the operator checks color and consistency during batch changeover.

---

## 5. SPC: Statistical Process Control

SPC helps distinguish natural variation from a problem signal. If a parameter fluctuates around the mean, the process may be stable. If a trend, jump or point outside control limits appears, the cause should be investigated.

![SPC process chart](/images/lectures/process-design/spc-control-chart.svg)

SPC is useful for net weight, temperature, moisture, pH, Brix, viscosity, holding time and reject rate. It does not replace a specification, but it shows whether the process is under control.

---

## 6. Deviations and Product Decisions

A deviation is not just a “bad number.” It is a situation where a decision is needed for both product and process.

![Deviation decision tree](/images/lectures/process-design/deviation-decision-tree.svg)

Typical steps are: stop or limit the risk, hold affected product, define the time window, check sensors and records, assess impact on safety, quality and labeling, decide on release, rework, additional testing or disposal, find the cause, apply corrective action and verify effectiveness.

Without product hold, a deviation quickly becomes a traceability problem.

---

## 7. Records and Responsibility

A record should show not only the result but also the decision. Quality records need parameter, method, time, batch, equipment, operator, result, limits, product status, deviation and action.

The plant should define who can release a batch, who can stop the line, who assesses risk and who may change a recipe or control limit.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes

- testing only finished product
- having specifications without actions for nonconformity
- sampling where the result cannot be used
- trusting online sensors without calibration
- confusing specification limits with SPC control limits
- not holding product during deviation
- recording a correction without a cause
- changing control limits so that results “look normal”
