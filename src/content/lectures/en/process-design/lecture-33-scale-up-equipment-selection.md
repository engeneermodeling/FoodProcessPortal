# Lecture 33. Scale-Up and Equipment Selection for Food Lines

## What You Will Learn

After this lecture you will be able to define design inputs, explain why scale-up is not simple enlargement, identify the bottleneck of a line, match equipment capacities, include utility systems and check a design for quality, safety, economics and controllability.

---

## 1. Design Starts With the Product

Equipment cannot be selected correctly without knowing the product. Food products differ in viscosity, density, solids content, particles, fat, proteins, sugars, temperature limits, foaming, sticking, oxidation, structure sensitivity and microbiological requirements.

![Design inputs](/images/lectures/process-design/design-inputs.svg)

The same machine may work well with water and poorly with sauce, yoghurt, puree or a suspension with particles. Nameplate capacity is therefore not always the real capacity for a specific product.

---

## 2. Required Input Data

Before selecting equipment, define the product, target capacity, operating mode, shift schedule, raw materials, losses, yield, allowed temperature and pressure, processing time, cleaning requirements, utilities, space limits, automation needs, data recording and safety requirements.

Without these inputs, design becomes guessing.

---

## 3. Balances and Capacity

Material and heat balances come first. The material balance shows how much raw material enters, how much product leaves and where losses occur. The heat balance shows how much heat must be supplied or removed.

Then equipment capacities are matched. A line cannot run faster than its bottleneck.

![Line capacity balance](/images/lectures/process-design/process-line-balance.svg)

If a pasteurizer can process 5 t/h but the filler can handle only 3 t/h, the line needs a buffer or a different operating strategy. If cleaning takes a long time, real daily capacity is lower than nameplate capacity.

---

## 4. Scale-Up

Scale-up is the transition from laboratory or pilot conditions to industrial operation. When size increases, more changes than volume and throughput.

![Scale-up risks](/images/lectures/process-design/scale-up-risks.svg)

Mixing time, heat-transfer coefficient, pressure drop, temperature distribution, particle damage, foaming, residence time, control inertia and start-up losses may all change. Pilot trials provide data that cannot be reliably guessed from a laboratory beaker.

---

## 5. Selecting Equipment

Equipment is selected not only by capacity. Check product suitability, temperature and pressure limits, hygienic execution, CIP cleanability, mechanical product damage, pressure drop, compatibility with the line, utility demand, installation space and access for service.

Good selection is a compromise between product quality, safety, capacity, cost and ease of operation.

---

## 6. Utilities and Layout

The line must be supported by steam or hot water, refrigeration, potable and technical water, compressed air, electricity, drainage, wastewater handling, CIP, ventilation or dust extraction and maintenance access.

Layout should account for raw material, product, staff, packaging, waste and cleaning flows. Clean and dirty zones should be separated, crossings minimized and access kept for cleaning and repair.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

- selecting a machine only by nameplate capacity
- ignoring cleaning, start-up and shutdown time
- not matching equipment capacities
- forgetting buffer tanks
- ignoring pressure drops in pipes and valves
- scaling geometry without checking heat transfer or mixing
- not checking utilities
- leaving no access for cleaning and maintenance
- not checking emergency shutdown and product rejection scenarios
