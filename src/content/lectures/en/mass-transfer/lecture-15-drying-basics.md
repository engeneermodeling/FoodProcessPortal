# Lecture 15. Drying of Food Materials

## Learning Outcomes

After this lecture you will be able to explain why foods are dried, distinguish wet-basis and dry-basis moisture, describe forms of water binding, read a drying-rate curve, perform a simple material balance and select a dryer type.

---

## 1. Why Foods Are Dried

Drying removes moisture by heat and mass transfer. The goal is shelf life, lower weight, easier transport and desired texture, not just "making it dry".

Examples include milk powder, whey powder, dried fruits, vegetables, herbs, cereals, snacks, coffee extracts and starch products.

---

## 2. Moisture Content

Wet basis:

```text
w = moisture mass / total mass
```

Dry basis:

```text
X = moisture mass / dry-solids mass
```

Dry basis is convenient in calculations because dry solids remain constant during drying.

---

## 3. Forms of Moisture

![Forms of moisture in a food material](/images/lectures/mass-transfer/moisture-forms.svg)

Surface water evaporates first, capillary water moves through pores, osmotically bound water is held by cell structure, and adsorbed water is the hardest to remove.

---

## 4. Drying Air and Drying Rate

Hot air supplies heat, accepts water vapour and removes it from the drying zone. Temperature, relative humidity, air velocity and recirculation determine drying intensity and product damage risk.

![Drying-rate curve](/images/lectures/mass-transfer/drying-rate-curve.svg)

Drying includes warm-up, constant-rate and falling-rate periods. Many foods quickly enter the falling-rate period where internal moisture diffusion is limiting.

---

## 5. Material Balance

```text
G_1 · (1 - w_1) = G_2 · (1 - w_2)
W = G_1 - G_2
```

Example: `1000 kg` apple slices at `80%` moisture are dried to `15%`.

```text
dry solids = 1000 · (1 - 0.80) = 200 kg
G_2 = 200 / (1 - 0.15) = 235.3 kg
W = 764.7 kg
```

---

## 6. Dryer Selection

![Dryer selection for food products](/images/lectures/mass-transfer/dryer-selection-map.svg)

| Dryer | Products |
|---|---|
| **Cabinet / tray** | fruits, vegetables, herbs, small batches |
| **Belt** | pieces, flakes, snacks, vegetables |
| **Drum** | pastes, purees, starch products |
| **Fluid bed** | granules, powders, crumbs |
| **Spray** | milk, whey, extracts, solutions |
| **Freeze dryer** | berries, premium or heat-sensitive ingredients |

Further reading: [Tetra Pak Dairy Processing Handbook: milk and whey powdered ingredients](https://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/milk-and-whey-powdered-ingredients) and [Bühler drying technology](https://www.buhlergroup.com/global/en/process-technologies/Drying.html).

---

## Key Takeaways

- Drying is both heat and mass transfer.
- Dry-solids balance is the first calculation.
- Bound water is slower and more expensive to remove.
- The falling-rate period is often decisive for foods.
- Dryer type depends on product form, heat sensitivity, moisture, quality and throughput.
