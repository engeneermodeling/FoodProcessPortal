# Lecture 17. Extraction of Food Raw Materials

## Learning Outcomes

After this lecture you will be able to explain extraction, identify the mass-transfer driving force, describe key process factors, compare single-stage, multistage and counter-current extraction, and choose a solvent with food safety in mind.

---

## 1. Principle

Extraction removes target compounds from raw material using a solvent. In foods, the compounds may be inside cells, pores or solid matrices, so diffusion is often as important as solubility.

![Principle of extraction from solid food raw material](/images/lectures/mass-transfer/extraction-principle.svg)

---

## 2. Driving Force

```text
Δc = c* - c
N = k · F · Δc
```

Extraction is faster when surface area, mixing and concentration difference increase, and when temperature and solvent are chosen correctly.

---

## 3. Process Factors

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Particle size** | smaller particles increase surface area but may clog filters |
| **Temperature** | increases diffusion and solubility but may damage aroma |
| **Contact time** | improves yield until equilibrium is approached |
| **Raw material / solvent ratio** | more solvent gives stronger driving force but costs more to concentrate |
| **Mixing** | reduces external mass-transfer resistance |
| **Pretreatment** | grinding, flaking and heating change accessibility |

---

## 4. Flow Schemes

![Extraction schemes](/images/lectures/mass-transfer/extraction-flow-schemes.svg)

Counter-current extraction is efficient because depleted solids contact fresh solvent while fresh solids contact enriched extract.

## Key Takeaways

- Extraction transfers target compounds from raw material into a solvent.
- The driving force is concentration difference.
- Particle size, temperature, time, mixing, solvent and raw-material structure control the rate.
- Counter-current systems improve yield with less solvent.
- Food extraction must balance yield, selectivity, solvent safety, aroma and extract clarity.
