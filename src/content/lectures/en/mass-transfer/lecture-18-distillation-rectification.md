# Lecture 18. Distillation and Rectification in Food Technologies

## Learning Outcomes

After this lecture you will be able to explain separation by volatility, distinguish simple distillation, steam distillation and rectification, describe a rectification column and identify food applications.

---

## 1. Principle

Distillation separates liquid mixtures because the vapour is richer in the more volatile component.

![Principle of simple distillation](/images/lectures/mass-transfer/distillation-principle.svg)

In food processing, distillation is used for aromas, essential oils, beverage dealcoholisation, solvent recovery and removal of unwanted volatiles.

---

## 2. Vapour-Liquid Equilibrium

For a volatile component `A`:

```text
y_A > x_A
```

where `x_A` is the liquid composition and `y_A` is the vapour composition. The larger the difference, the easier the separation.

---

## 3. Rectification

Rectification repeatedly contacts rising vapour with descending liquid in a column.

![Rectification column](/images/lectures/mass-transfer/rectification-column.svg)

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Reboiler** | generates vapour |
| **Column** | provides vapour-liquid contact |
| **Trays / packing** | increase mass-transfer area |
| **Condenser** | condenses overhead vapour |
| **Reflux** | returns part of condensate to improve separation |
| **Distillate** | top product enriched in volatile component |

More reflux usually means purer distillate but higher energy demand.

---

## 4. Food Applications

![Food applications of distillation and rectification](/images/lectures/mass-transfer/food-distillation-examples.svg)

| Application | Key issue |
|---|---|
| **Beverage dealcoholisation** | remove alcohol while preserving flavour |
| **Essential oils** | gentle recovery of aroma compounds |
| **Aroma distillates** | selective recovery from juices, herbs and spices |
| **Solvent recovery** | safe reuse or removal of ethanol and extractants |
| **Alcoholic mixtures** | fraction control and quality |

## Key Takeaways

- Distillation separates components by volatility.
- Simple distillation is rough; rectification gives repeated enrichment.
- Reflux improves purity but increases energy use.
- Food applications require aroma protection, mild temperature, safe materials and residue control.
