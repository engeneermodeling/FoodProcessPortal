# Lecture 20. Crystallization in Food Technology

## Learning Outcomes

After this lecture you will be able to:

- explain supersaturation
- distinguish nucleation and crystal growth
- describe seeding, cooling and agitation
- relate crystal size to food texture
- use examples from sugar, lactose, salt, ice cream, fats and concentrates

---

## 1. Why Crystallization Matters

Crystallization transfers a component from solution, melt or a supercooled system into a solid crystalline phase. In food processing it affects yield, texture, flowability, stability and appearance.

Examples include sucrose crystals in sugar manufacture, lactose in condensed milk and whey products, ice in frozen desserts, fat crystals in chocolate and butter, and salt crystals in dry mixes or brines.

![Crystal size and food quality](/images/lectures/mass-transfer/crystal-size-food-quality.svg)

---

## 2. Supersaturation

Crystallization requires a concentration above the equilibrium solubility:

```text
S = C / C*
```

`S` is supersaturation ratio, `C` is actual concentration, and `C*` is the saturated concentration.

If `S < 1`, crystals dissolve. At moderate supersaturation existing crystals grow. At high supersaturation many new nuclei may appear, making the process harder to control.

![Crystallization zones](/images/lectures/mass-transfer/crystallization-zones.svg)

---

## 3. Nucleation and Growth

Nucleation creates new crystals. Growth enlarges existing crystals. Food quality often depends on the size distribution, not only on the total amount of solid phase. Large lactose crystals cause sandy condensed milk; large ice crystals make ice cream coarse; sugar production requires crystals large and strong enough for separation.

---

## 4. Creating Supersaturation

Common methods are cooling, evaporation, vacuum flash cooling, addition of a component that lowers solubility, changing composition, or freezing part of the water.

In dairy concentrates, evaporation, cooling and seeding are often combined to create many controlled growth centres.

![Controlled crystallization process](/images/lectures/mass-transfer/crystallizer-process.svg)

---

## 5. Seeding and Equipment

Seed crystals provide controlled growth sites. They must be fine, clean, added at the right temperature and distributed evenly. Agitation should prevent gradients but avoid excessive crystal breakage.

A crystallizer may include a tank or continuous vessel, cooling or evaporation system, agitator, temperature profile control, seeding point and discharge to a centrifuge, filter or dryer.

---

## Food Examples

Condensed milk needs small lactose crystals to avoid sandiness. Whey concentrate is crystallized before drying to reduce hygroscopicity and improve lactose recovery. Ice cream requires fine ice crystals. Chocolate and fats need controlled fat polymorphs for gloss, snap and bloom resistance.
