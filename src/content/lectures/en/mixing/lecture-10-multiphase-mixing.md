# Lecture 10. Mixing of Multiphase Food Systems

## Learning Outcomes

After this lecture you will be able to distinguish solid-liquid, liquid-liquid and gas-liquid mixing tasks, explain complete particle suspension, describe how agitation affects emulsion droplets, and decide whether foam is a desired structure or a process defect.

---

## 1. Why Multiphase Mixing Is Harder

In a single liquid, mixing mainly equalises composition or temperature. In a multiphase food system, the agitator must also control a second phase: particles settle, droplets coalesce, bubbles rise and foam may overflow.

![Multiphase mixing in food systems](/images/lectures/mixing/multiphase-mixing-map.svg)

| System | Examples | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Solid + liquid** | cocoa drinks, spices in marinade, tomato pulp | sediment, non-uniform solids dose |
| **Liquid + liquid** | mayonnaise, sauces, dairy-fat mixtures | phase separation, large droplets |
| **Gas + liquid** | aerated desserts, beer, carbonated drinks | uncontrolled foam or gas loss |

---

## 2. Suspensions

For a suspension, the aim is not only to disturb sediment but to keep particles from resting on the bottom for long periods. This state is called complete suspension.

![States of solid particle suspension](/images/lectures/mixing/suspension-states.svg)

Suspension depends on particle size and density, density difference, liquid viscosity, impeller diameter, clearance from the bottom, axial flow and vessel-bottom shape. Cocoa, spices and pulp often need strong axial circulation. If particles are fragile, excessive shear can damage texture.

---

## 3. Emulsions

An emulsion contains two immiscible liquids. The agitator breaks one phase into droplets, but stability also needs formulation: emulsifier, proteins, stabilisers and continuous-phase viscosity.

![Effect of mixing intensity on emulsion droplets](/images/lectures/mixing/emulsion-droplets.svg)

More shear usually means smaller droplets and slower separation, but excessive shear can heat the product or damage texture. Without stabilisation, droplets may coalesce again after mixing.

---

## 4. Gas-Liquid Systems and Foam

| Goal | Example | Engineering response |
|---|---|---|
| **introduce gas** | aerated dessert, whipped cream | small bubbles and stable foam |
| **retain CO₂** | carbonated drinks | reduce unnecessary agitation |
| **remove air** | syrup before filling | deaeration, vacuum, gentle mixing |
| **avoid foam** | brines, protein solutions, CIP | reduce vortexing and air entrainment |

Foam is desirable in whipped desserts but undesirable in syrups, pump lines and filling operations.

---

## Product-Based Selection

| Product | Practical approach |
|---|---|
| **sugar syrup** | propeller or paddle, temperature control |
| **tomato pulp** | axial flow, no bottom dead zones |
| **mayonnaise / sauce** | turbine or high-shear zone, controlled oil addition |
| **jam / cream** | anchor or frame agitator with scrapers |
| **drink with particles** | gentle suspension without damaging inclusions |
| **aerated dessert** | bubble size and foam stability control |

Industrial background: [Tetra Pak Dairy Processing Handbook: Mixers](https://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/mixers).

---

## Key Takeaways

- Multiphase mixing controls particles, droplets or bubbles.
- Suspensions require complete suspension and no persistent sediment.
- Emulsions need shear, stabilisation, temperature control and proper phase addition.
- Foam can be a product structure or a defect.
- Agitator choice belongs to the product, not only to tank volume.
