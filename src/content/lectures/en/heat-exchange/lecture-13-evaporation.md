# Lecture 13. Evaporation and Concentration of Food Products

## Learning Outcomes

After this lecture you will be able to distinguish heating, evaporation and drying, perform a solids balance, estimate evaporated water, explain vacuum evaporation, describe multiple-effect evaporators and recognise quality risks.

---

## 1. Why Foods Are Concentrated

Evaporation removes part of the solvent, usually water, while the final product remains liquid or paste-like. It is used for milk before drying, whey, fruit and vegetable juices, tomato paste, purees, sugar syrups and extracts.

Concentration reduces volume and transport cost, but increases viscosity, burn-on risk and possible aroma loss.

---

## 2. Principle

![Principle of evaporating a food liquid](/images/lectures/heat-exchange/evaporation-principle.svg)

Steam condenses on one side of a heat-transfer surface while the product boils on the other side. The main heat-transfer equation is still:

```text
Q = K · F · Δt
```

but much of the heat is used for phase change.

---

## 3. Solids Balance

```text
G_f · x_f = G_c · x_c
W = G_f - G_c
```

Example: `1000 kg/h` juice at `12%` solids is concentrated to `45%`.

```text
G_c = 1000 · 0.12 / 0.45 = 266.7 kg/h
W = 1000 - 266.7 = 733.3 kg/h
```

---

## 4. Vacuum, Multiple Effects and Recompression

Vacuum lowers boiling temperature and helps protect flavour, colour, proteins and vitamins.

![Multiple-effect evaporation](/images/lectures/heat-exchange/multiple-effect-evaporation.svg)

In multiple-effect evaporation, vapour from one effect heats the next effect at lower pressure. TVR uses motive steam to recompress vapour; MVR uses a mechanical fan or compressor.

Further reading: [Tetra Pak Dairy Processing Handbook: Evaporators](https://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/evaporators) and [GEA evaporation plants](https://www.gea.com/en/products/evaporators-crystallizers/evaporator-plants/?i=food).

---

## 5. Product Quality

![Quality risks during evaporation](/images/lectures/heat-exchange/evaporation-quality-risks.svg)

Engineers monitor not only total solids but also colour, cooked flavour, aroma loss, viscosity increase, foam entrainment and fouling.

| Product | Main concerns |
|---|---|
| **juice** | aroma and colour |
| **milk** | protein heat damage and cooked flavour |
| **tomato paste** | viscosity, colour and burn-on |
| **syrup** | crystallisation and surface cleanliness |

---

## Key Takeaways

- Evaporation removes water while solids remain in the concentrate.
- The first calculation is `G_f x_f = G_c x_c`.
- Vacuum reduces boiling temperature and protects quality.
- Multiple effects, TVR and MVR save energy.
- Concentrate quality depends on temperature, residence time, foam, viscosity, fouling and aroma retention.
