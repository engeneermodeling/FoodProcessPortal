# Lecture 12. Heat Exchangers for Food Processing

## Learning Outcomes

After this lecture you will be able to distinguish plate, tubular, scraped-surface and jacketed heat exchangers, select equipment from product properties, explain heat regeneration, and account for fouling, CIP and pressure drop.

---

## 1. No Universal Heat Exchanger

Milk, clear juice, tomato puree, mayonnaise, jam and caramel filling have different viscosity, particles, burn-on tendency and heat sensitivity.

![Main heat exchanger types for food processing](/images/lectures/heat-exchange/heat-exchanger-types.svg)

| Type | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Plate** | high `K`, compactness, regeneration | not ideal for large particles or heavy fouling |
| **Tubular** | better passage for particles | larger area and footprint |
| **Scraped-surface** | viscous and burn-on-prone products | mechanically complex and expensive |
| **Jacketed vessel** | simple batch heating | slower heat transfer and local overheating risk |

---

## 2. Plate Heat Exchangers

Plate heat exchangers use thin corrugated plates. Hot and cold streams pass through neighbouring channels without mixing. Corrugation increases turbulence and heat transfer.

They are suitable for milk, low-viscosity cream, beer, clear juices and process water. Their advantages are compactness, high heat-transfer coefficient, heat regeneration, CIP and modular area. Their weaknesses are narrow channels, gasket limits and fouling sensitivity.

---

## 3. Heat Regeneration

![Heat regeneration in a plate pasteurisation system](/images/lectures/heat-exchange/plate-regeneration.svg)

Hot pasteurised product preheats cold incoming product. This reduces both steam demand and cooling demand. Further reading: [Tetra Pak Dairy Processing Handbook: Heat exchangers](https://dairyprocessinghandbook.tetrapak.com/chapter/heat-exchangers).

---

## 4. Tubular and Scraped-Surface Exchangers

Tubular heat exchangers are used when particles, fibres, pulp or higher viscosity make plate channels risky. Typical examples are tomato products, fruit purees, pulpy juices and sauces.

Scraped-surface heat exchangers continuously remove product from the wall. They are used for viscous, sticky or burn-on-prone products such as jams, creams, pastes, caramel masses and processed cheese.

---

## 5. Fouling and CIP

![Fouling and CIP cycle](/images/lectures/heat-exchange/fouling-cip-cycle.svg)

During operation, protein, mineral, fat or sugar deposits add thermal resistance and reduce `K`. Signs include lower product outlet temperature, higher steam or cooling demand, increased pressure drop and recovery after cleaning.

CIP is part of the production cycle, not an optional accessory.

---

## Product-Based Selection

| Product | Likely choice |
|---|---|
| **milk for pasteurisation** | plate system with regeneration |
| **tomato puree** | tubular or scraped-surface exchanger |
| **jam** | scraped-surface exchanger or jacketed vessel with scraping |
| **clear juice** | plate exchanger |
| **sauce with particles** | tubular, depending on particle size |

---

## Key Takeaways

- Heat exchanger selection starts with the product.
- Plate units are efficient for clean low-viscosity products.
- Tubular units handle particles and higher viscosity better.
- Scraped-surface units are for sticky, viscous and burn-on-prone systems.
- Regeneration saves energy; CIP keeps the real `K` from collapsing.
